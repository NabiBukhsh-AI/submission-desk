# Submission Desk, on one page

For the person doing the screening. Print this.

## What it does

You give it a candidate's CV and any supporting documents. It reads them
against the list of things the role needs, and shows you what the documents
actually say about each point — quoted, with the page number, so you can check
every line yourself.

It then works out a recommendation using a fixed set of rules you can read,
and tells you which rules fired and why.

## What it does not do

It does not decide. You do. Nothing is sent to anybody until you say so.

It does not guess. If the documents never mention something, it says the
documents never mentioned it. That is different from saying the candidate
cannot do it, and the system keeps the two apart.

It never mentions age, gender, nationality, ethnicity, religion, family,
disability, appearance, or "culture fit". If you ever see any of those in
what it shows you, that is a fault. Report it.

## The three questions it answers

**Can I trust these documents?** The coloured bar at the top. Green: nothing
unusual. Amber: something worth a look, and it says what. Red: the document
contained text written to trick an automated reader. You see the exact text.
Nothing was assessed, and it is your call.

**What does it think, and why?** The outcome with what it means, the reasons
a person is needed with what to do about each, then every rule that fired, in
sentences. "Not enough information" is not a bad mark. It means the documents
did not cover enough of the role to judge, and the questions further down say
what to ask for. "Check with the candidate first" means one requirement that
decides on its own — usually work authorisation — was not answered either way.
Every word on the page is defined at the bottom of it.

**Where did it get that?** Every point expands to show the quotations behind
it. "Show me where" puts a quotation back into its paragraph. A separate
section shows quotations the system could not find in the document — the
system checking its own work. Empty is good.

## When you disagree

Open **Correct an assessment**, change what the point should have been, and
say why from the list. You never type a score. The recommendation is
recalculated with the same rules, and you see the new one before you commit.

The reason matters. Each one goes somewhere different: to whoever tunes the
reading, to the person who owns the role's requirements, or into the list of
things the system is known not to know.

## Your decision

**Approve** — the package is ready to send. Sending is a separate step.
**Ask for more information** — the requests go to the candidate.
**Do not proceed** — nothing is sent.

The trust slider is not a formality. It is one of the two numbers used to
decide whether this system is worth keeping.

## Three things to know

The requirements for the role are on the **Roles** page, where you can read
and change them. Changing them changes how every future candidate is
assessed; candidates already reviewed keep the version they were reviewed
under. Ticking candidates in the queue lets you run them against another
role without uploading again.

If two people open the same candidate and both decide, the second is asked to
reload and read the first decision before making theirs.

Anything that looks wrong — a quotation that is not in the CV, a question
about something the CV clearly answers, reasoning that does not follow —
correct it first, so it is recorded, and then report it.
